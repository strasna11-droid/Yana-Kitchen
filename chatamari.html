The Story Behind the Dish

More Than Just a Crepe

Chatamari has been part of Newari culture for as long as anyone can remember. It is prepared during festivals, offered at ceremonies, and served at family gatherings where food is meant to be shared slowly and with care.

The name comes from the Newari language, and the dish is so central to Newari identity that it is sometimes called the Newari pizza — a comparison that makes visitors smile and then immediately want to taste it for themselves.

What makes Chatamari special is its simplicity. Rice flour, water, a little mustard oil, and whatever the season offers on top. In our kitchen you will make it the way it has always been made — no shortcuts, no substitutions, just the real thing.

The Process

How to Make Chatamari

These are the steps you will follow during the class, guided the whole way through.

1
Make the batter

Mix rice flour with water gradually, stirring until completely smooth with no lumps. The batter should be thin enough to pour and spread easily. Add a pinch of salt, stir, and set aside to rest for five minutes.

2
Season the meat

Combine the minced meat with garlic, ginger, turmeric, cumin, timur, and salt. Mix well until everything is evenly incorporated. This is where the real Newari flavour comes in — do not rush it.

3
Heat the pan

Place a flat, heavy-bottomed pan on medium-low heat. Brush the surface lightly with mustard oil. The oil should shimmer but not smoke — getting the temperature right is the key to a good Chatamari.

4
Pour and spread the batter

Pour a small ladle of batter onto the centre of the pan and spread it into a thin circle using the back of the ladle. Work quickly and with confidence. Cook on low heat until the edges begin to lift and the surface looks dry.

5
Add the toppings

Spread a thin, even layer of seasoned meat across the surface of the crepe. Make a small well in the centre and crack an egg into it. The egg will cook gently on top of the meat without needing to flip the Chatamari.

6
Cover and finish

Place a lid over the pan and cook on low heat until the egg white is fully set but the yolk is still slightly soft. This takes around three to four minutes. The base should be golden and just starting to crisp.

7
Garnish and serve

Scatter fresh coriander over the top and serve directly from the pan. Chatamari is best eaten immediately while the base is still crisp. Slide it onto a plate, sit down together, and eat.
